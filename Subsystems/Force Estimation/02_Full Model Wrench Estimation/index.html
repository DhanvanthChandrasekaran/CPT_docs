Full Model Wrench Estimation

⚠️ Note: This documentation is the not final draft and is AI-generated. It provides a detailed explanation based on the mathematical models and theory presented in the research paper "Robust Collaborative Object Transportation Using Multiple MAVs" (arXiv:1711.08753v1) by Andrea Tagliabue, Mina Kamel, Roland Siegwart, and Juan Nieto[cite: 1].

1. Introduction and Theory

The Full Model Wrench Estimation framework leverages the complete, nonlinear rotational and translational rigid body dynamics of a Micro Aerial Vehicle (MAV). This method assumes access to the individual rotational speeds of the MAV's propellers, allowing for high-fidelity modeling of actuator torques and aerodynamic blade flapping effects.

To overcome the inherent singularities of Euler angles (e.g., Gimbal lock), the MAV's attitude is strictly parameterized via a unit quaternion. Because directly filtering a constrained unit quaternion can break its unitary constraint and result in singular covariance matrices, this approach utilizes an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) integrated with the Unscented Quaternion Estimator (USQUE) methodology, which temporarily maps quaternions to non-singular Modified Rodrigues Parameters (MRPs) during the error-covariance computation.

2. Reference Frames and Variable Definitions

2.1 Variables and Dimensions

  • \({}_I p, {}_I v \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 1}\): Position and velocity in the inertial frame \(I\).
  • \(q = [q_v^T, q_s]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 1}\): Unitary attitude quaternion mapping frame \(B\) to \(I\).
  • \(R_{IB}(q) \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}\): Rotation matrix derived from the quaternion \(q\).
  • \({}_B \omega \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 1}\): Angular velocity of the MAV, expressed in the body frame \(B\).
  • \(m \in \mathbb{R}\): Mass of the MAV \([kg]\).
  • \({}_B J \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}\): Diagonal moment of inertia tensor in \(B\) \([kg \cdot m^2]\).
  • \(F_{cmd} \in \mathbb{R}\): Total scalar commanded thrust \([N]\).
  • \({}_B F^{aero} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 1}\): Aerodynamic drag forces acting on the body \([N]\).
  • \({}_I F^{ext} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 1}\): Unknown external force expressed in \(I\) \([N]\).
  • \({}_B M^{prop} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 1}\): Total control torque generated by the actuators in \(B\) \([N \cdot m]\).
  • \({}_B M_z^{ext} \in \mathbb{R}\): Unknown external torque around the \(z\)-axis. (To reduce computational load, \(x\) and \(y\) external torques are often excluded).
  • \(u = [n_1, \dots, n_6]^T \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 1}\): System input consisting of individual rotor speeds \([rad \cdot s^{-1}]\).

3. Full System Modeling

3.1 Translational Dynamics

The linear acceleration incorporates detailed aerodynamic body and blade-flapping effects: $$ {}I \dot{p} = {}_I v $$ $$ {}_I \dot{v} = \frac{1}{m} R{IB}(q) \left( \begin{bmatrix} 0 \ 0 \ F_{cmd} \end{bmatrix} - {}B F^{aero} \right) + \frac{1}{m} {}_I F^{ext} - {}_I g $$ Where aerodynamic drag is lumped as: $$ {}_B F^{aero} = \begin{bmatrix} k{drag} \sum_{i=1}^6 n_i & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}^T $$

3.2 Rotational Dynamics

The angular acceleration is driven by the Euler equations for rigid body dynamics: $$ {}_B \dot{\omega} = {}_B J^{-1} \left( {}_B M^{prop} - {}_B \omega \times {}_B J {}_B \omega + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \ 0 \ {}_B M_z^{ext} \end{bmatrix} \right) $$ Explanation: The change in angular velocity considers the gyroscopic effects \(({}_B \omega \times {}_B J {}_B \omega)\), the explicit torque generated by the propellers, and the external unmeasured torque.

4. UKF and USQUE Filter Formulation

4.1 State and Covariance Parameterization

A standard Kalman Filter state vector using a quaternion has 17 elements. However, to maintain a non-singular \(16 \times 16\) covariance matrix, the error state \(\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times 1}\) is defined using a 3-dimensional attitude error vector \(\epsilon\) (Modified Rodrigues Parameters):

  • Filter Error State Vector \(\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{16 \times 1}\): $$ \xi = \begin{bmatrix} {}_I p^T & {}_I v^T & \epsilon^T & {}_B \omega^T & {}_I F^{ext,T} & {}_B M_z^{ext} \end{bmatrix}^T $$
  • Measurement Vector \(z \in \mathbb{R}^{12 \times 1}\): $$ z = \begin{bmatrix} p^{m,T} & v^{m,T} & \epsilon^{m,T} & \omega^{m,T} \end{bmatrix}^T $$

4.2 UKF Prediction Step (A Priori)

  1. Sigma Point Generation: Generate \(2n+1\) (\(n=16\)) sigma points \(\hat{\xi}_{k-1}^{(i)}\) using the state covariance matrix \(P_{k-1}^+\).
  2. MRP to Quaternion Conversion: Extract the attitude error part \(\hat{\epsilon}_{k-1}^{(i)}\) from each sigma point. Convert it to a delta quaternion \(\delta\hat{q}_{k-1}^{(i)}\) using the MRP mapping. The quaternion sigma points are generated via quaternion multiplication: \(\hat{q}_{k-1}^{(i)} = \delta\hat{q}_{k-1}^{(i)} \otimes \hat{q}_{k-1}^+\).
  3. Propagation: Pass all complete sigma points (using \(\hat{q}\) instead of \(\epsilon\)) through the non-linear process models (Section 3). The quaternion is integrated via: $$ q_{k} = \Omega({}B \omega{k-1}) q_{k-1} $$ (where \(\Omega\) is the quaternion update matrix based on angular velocity).
  4. Mean and Covariance Re-calculation: Extract the propagated delta quaternions, convert back to propagated MRP error vectors \(\hat{\epsilon}_k^{(i)}\), and compute the predicted mean \(\hat{\xi}_k^-\) and raw covariance \(P_{k, prereset}^-\).
  5. Covariance Reset: Apply the USQUE covariance correction step to account for the moving reference of the attitude error: $$ P_k^- = T_{reset}(\hat{\epsilon}k^-) P{k, prereset}^- T_{reset}(\hat{\epsilon}_k^-)^T $$ where \(T_{reset}(\hat{\epsilon}_k^-) = diag(I_{6 \times 6}, R(\frac{1}{2}\hat{\epsilon}_k^-), I_{7 \times 7})\).

4.3 UKF Update Step (A Posteriori)

Since the measurement model maps the state directly to the sensors, it behaves linearly: 1. Kalman Gain: Calculate the standard gain \(K_k = P_k^- H^T (H P_k^- H^T + R)^{-1}\). 2. State Update: Correct the error state: \(\hat{\xi}_k^+ = \hat{\xi}_k^- + K_k (z_k - H \hat{\xi}_k^-)\). The measurement \(z_k\) maps the measured quaternion \(q_k^m\) into the MRP error representation \(\epsilon_k^m\) relative to the predicted quaternion. 3. Attitude Update: Extract \(\hat{\epsilon}_k^+\) from \(\hat{\xi}_k^+\), convert it to \(\delta\hat{q}_k^+\), and finally update the full MAV attitude estimate: $$ \hat{q}_k^+ = \delta\hat{q}_k^+ \otimes \hat{q}_k^- $$ 4. Covariance Update & Reset: Compute \(P_k^+ = (I - K_k H) P_k^-\) and apply the \(T_{reset}\) operator once more to finalize the step, yielding optimal estimates for the external wrench forces and torques.
